Literature Search

A vetted literature review for FORGE, spanning the methods the platform must implement and the prior art it positions against. Every entry below was confirmed against arXiv, the official proceedings (PMLR / NeurIPS / JMLR / OpenReview), or the publisher of record (IEEE / MIT Press / AAAI / Springer / PLOS). The set is organized into six themes — inference, Laplace, sampling, calibration, visualization, and survey — followed by the experiment & domain sources that anchor the three mk_1 reproductions.

Synthesis

The foundational spine is MacKay 1992 (Laplace), Neal 2011 and Hoffman & Gelman 2014 (HMC/NUTS), and the 2015–2017 inference trio (Blundell, Gal, Lakshminarayanan) plus Guo and Kendall & Gal on calibration — these define the methods and metrics any posterior tool must implement. The state-of-the-art cluster is Kristiadi 2020 and Daxberger 2021 (making last-layer Laplace both theoretically justified and real-time, i.e. FORGE’s fast path), Izmailov 2021 and Wilson & Izmailov 2020 (what true posteriors look like and why marginalization/geometry matters), and the 2023–2024 syntheses (Gawlikowski, Papamarkou). The open gap is striking: the visualization literature (Hohman, Wexler, Hullman) probes point predictions, fairness, and static uncertainty displays, while the BDL literature produces posteriors no one can interactively explore — there is no tool that couples a closed-form fast path (LLLA) to a live geometric/calibration interface where a hyperparameter knob reshapes the epistemic profile in real time, validated against HMC ground truth across calibration→benchmark→physics tiers. That coupling — faithful (H1), correct/efficient (H2), and knob-reactive (H3) over a multi-domain anchor like HiggsML — is precisely the unoccupied space FORGE targets.

Verification & caveats. Nothing here is fabricated or from a predatory venue. Where proceedings page numbers were not independently confirmed (several NeurIPS entries), they are omitted rather than guessed and the entry is cited by its proceedings / arXiv identifier. Neal 2011 is a book chapter (start page 113 confirmed via ADS bibcode; end page is the commonly-cited value). Two title variants are flagged inline (Blundell — singular “Network” in the proceedings; Papamarkou — arXiv vs. published title). Papamarkou 2024 is a peer-reviewed position paper, not an empirical study; Gawlikowski circulated as a 2021 preprint before its 2023 journal version.

Inference — variational · MC-dropout · ensembles

  1. inference

    Blundell, C., Cornebise, J., Kavukcuoglu, K. & Wierstra, D. (2015). Weight Uncertainty in Neural Network. ICML 2015, PMLR 37:1613–1622. PMLR · arXiv:1505.05424 proceedings title is singular “Network”; arXiv uses plural

    Contributes Bayes by Backprop — a backpropagation-compatible variational scheme learning a Gaussian over every weight via the reparameterization trick.

    For FORGE The canonical mean-field VI baseline a posterior explorer must support, anchoring the diagonal-Gaussian end of the fidelity spectrum.

  2. inference

    Gal, Y. & Ghahramani, Z. (2016). Dropout as a Bayesian Approximation: Representing Model Uncertainty in Deep Learning. ICML 2016, PMLR 48:1050–1059. PMLR · arXiv:1506.02142

    Contributes Shows test-time dropout approximates Bayesian inference in a deep Gaussian process, yielding uncertainty with essentially no training change.

    For FORGE A near-free uncertainty source exposable as a knob (dropout rate, sample count) for real-time MC predictive bands.

  3. inference

    Lakshminarayanan, B., Pritzel, A. & Blundell, C. (2017). Simple and Scalable Predictive Uncertainty Estimation using Deep Ensembles. NeurIPS 2017 (Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 30). NeurIPS · arXiv:1612.01474 proceedings page numbers not independently confirmed

    Contributes A non-Bayesian ensemble of independently trained nets that matches or beats approximate BNNs on calibration and out-of-distribution behavior.

    For FORGE The strong empirical baseline every uncertainty profile is judged against; later recast as approximate Bayesian marginalization (Wilson & Izmailov 2020).

Laplace approximation — including last-layer (LLLA)

  1. Laplace

    MacKay, D.J.C. (1992). A Practical Bayesian Framework for Backpropagation Networks. Neural Computation 4(3):448–472. DOI:10.1162/neco.1992.4.3.448

    Contributes The foundational Laplace/“evidence” framework for neural nets — Gaussian posterior around a mode, marginal-likelihood model comparison, effective number of parameters.

    For FORGE The theoretical root of the LLLA fast path; its marginal-likelihood machinery underpins any principled hyperparameter knob.

  2. Laplace

    Ritter, H., Botev, A. & Barber, D. (2018). A Scalable Laplace Approximation for Neural Networks. ICLR 2018. OpenReview (Skdvd2xAZ) conference uses OpenReview, not page numbers

    Contributes A Kronecker-factored (KFAC) Laplace posterior computed post-hoc on a trained net — a matrix-normal weight distribution with no retraining.

    For FORGE The structured-covariance tier between diagonal Laplace and full Hessian — a natural fidelity rung in the knob panel.

  3. Laplace

    Kristiadi, A., Hein, M. & Hennig, P. (2020). Being Bayesian, Even Just a Bit, Fixes Overconfidence in ReLU Networks. ICML 2020, PMLR 119:5436–5446. PMLR · arXiv:2002.10118

    Contributes Proves a cheap last-layer Gaussian (Laplace) removes the asymptotic overconfidence of ReLU MAP networks — “a bit Bayesian” suffices for calibration.

    For FORGE The theoretical license for the last-layer-only fast path: it makes real-time LLLA more than a shortcut.

  4. Laplace

    Daxberger, E., Kristiadi, A., Immer, A., Eschenhagen, R., Bauer, M. & Hennig, P. (2021). Laplace Redux — Effortless Bayesian Deep Learning. NeurIPS 2021 (vol. 34), 20089–20103. NeurIPS · arXiv:2106.14806

    Contributes Surveys LA variants (incl. last-layer, KFAC) and ships the laplace PyTorch library, showing LA is competitive with ensembles/VI at far lower cost.

    For FORGE The closest existing tooling to the FORGE backend; its closed-form last-layer predictive is exactly the real-time engine the knob panel needs.

Sampling & posterior geometry — HMC / NUTS

  1. sampling

    Neal, R.M. (2011). MCMC using Hamiltonian Dynamics. Ch. 5 in Handbook of Markov Chain Monte Carlo (Brooks, Gelman, Jones & Meng, eds.), Chapman & Hall/CRC, 113–162. DOI:10.1201/b10905 · arXiv:1206.1901

    Contributes The definitive exposition of HMC — momentum augmentation, leapfrog integration, volume preservation — making gradient-informed sampling of high-dimensional posteriors practical.

    For FORGE The reference method for the “gold-standard” posterior against which approximate-inference faithfulness (H2) is benchmarked.

  2. sampling

    Hoffman, M.D. & Gelman, A. (2014). The No-U-Turn Sampler: Adaptively Setting Path Lengths in Hamiltonian Monte Carlo. JMLR 15(47):1593–1623. JMLR · arXiv:1111.4246

    Contributes NUTS — automates HMC’s trajectory length and step size, enabling turnkey gradient sampling (the engine inside Stan / NumPyro / PyMC).

    For FORGE The practical sampler the ground-truth posteriors are actually drawn with; self-tuning makes reproducible reference runs feasible.

  3. sampling

    Izmailov, P., Vikram, S., Hoffman, M.D. & Wilson, A.G. (2021). What Are Bayesian Neural Network Posteriors Really Like? ICML 2021, PMLR vol. 139. PMLR · arXiv:2104.14421 proceedings page numbers not independently confirmed

    Contributes Uses full-batch HMC on modern architectures to characterize true BNN posteriors — finds cold-posterior effects are largely a data-augmentation artifact and BNNs generalize poorly under shift.

    For FORGE The empirical reference for what a faithful posterior visualization (H1) should reproduce, and a caution against over-trusting tempering knobs.

  4. sampling · geometry

    Wilson, A.G. & Izmailov, P. (2020). Bayesian Deep Learning and a Probabilistic Perspective of Generalization. NeurIPS 2020 (vol. 33). NeurIPS · arXiv:2002.08791 proceedings page numbers not independently confirmed

    Contributes Frames marginalization (not the prior) as the essence of Bayes, recasts deep ensembles as approximate BMA, and proposes MultiSWAG to marginalize within basins.

    For FORGE The conceptual bridge for the geometric-exploration framing — multimodality and basin structure are exactly what a posterior observatory should make visible.

Calibration & predictive-uncertainty evaluation

  1. calibration

    Pakdaman Naeini, M., Cooper, G.F. & Hauskrecht, M. (2015). Obtaining Well Calibrated Probabilities Using Bayesian Binning. AAAI 2015 (29th), 2901–2907. DOI:10.1609/aaai.v29i1.9602

    Contributes Introduces Bayesian Binning into Quantiles (BBQ) and the binned-calibration framing from which Expected Calibration Error (ECE) descends.

    For FORGE The origin of the binned-reliability machinery the calibration panel renders; the right citation when defining ECE rigorously.

  2. calibration

    Guo, C., Pleiss, G., Sun, Y. & Weinberger, K.Q. (2017). On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks. ICML 2017, PMLR 70:1321–1330. PMLR · arXiv:1706.04599

    Contributes Documents systematic miscalibration of modern deep nets and shows single-parameter temperature scaling fixes most of it; popularized ECE and reliability diagrams.

    For FORGE Defines the reliability-diagram + ECE views and the temperature knob that belong directly in the calibration tab.

  3. calibration

    Kendall, A. & Gal, Y. (2017). What Uncertainties Do We Need in Bayesian Deep Learning for Computer Vision? NeurIPS 2017 (vol. 30). NeurIPS · arXiv:1703.04977 proceedings page numbers not independently confirmed

    Contributes Operationalizes the aleatoric/epistemic decomposition in deep nets, combining input-dependent observation noise with weight-posterior uncertainty under one loss.

    For FORGE The reference for the decomposition view — separating reducible (epistemic, knob-sensitive) from irreducible (aleatoric) uncertainty is central to reading the H3 panel.

  4. calibration

    Ovadia, Y., Fertig, E., Ren, J., Nado, Z., Sculley, D., Nowozin, S., Dillon, J.V., Lakshminarayanan, B. & Snoek, J. (2019). Can You Trust Your Model’s Uncertainty? Evaluating Predictive Uncertainty Under Dataset Shift. NeurIPS 2019 (vol. 32). NeurIPS · arXiv:1906.02530 proceedings page numbers not independently confirmed

    Contributes A large-scale benchmark showing post-hoc calibration degrades under distributional shift while ensembles/SVI hold up better — establishing shift as the meaningful test.

    For FORGE The template for the shift-stress view; argues evaluation should sweep corruption intensity, not just report i.i.d. ECE.

Interactive & explorable visualization

  1. visualization

    Hullman, J., Resnick, P. & Adar, E. (2015). Hypothetical Outcome Plots Outperform Error Bars and Violin Plots for Inferences about Reliability of Variable Ordering. PLOS ONE 10(11):e0142444. DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0142444

    Contributes Empirically shows animated draws (HOPs) let non-experts read distributions more accurately than static error bars or violins.

    For FORGE Direct evidence for H1 — favours animating posterior draws over plotting credible-interval bands alone.

  2. visualization · survey

    Hohman, F., Kahng, M., Pienta, R. & Chau, D.H. (2019). Visual Analytics in Deep Learning: An Interrogative Survey for the Next Frontiers. IEEE TVCG 25(8):2674–2693. DOI:10.1109/TVCG.2018.2843369 · arXiv:1801.06889

    Contributes Organizes the DL-visualization landscape via a Why/Who/What/How/When/Where framework and maps open gaps.

    For FORGE The design-space survey to position FORGE within — and to cite for what existing tools do not cover, namely posterior geometry.

  3. visualization

    Wexler, J., Pushkarna, M., Bolukbasi, T., Wattenberg, M., Viégas, F. & Wilson, J. (2020). The What-If Tool: Interactive Probing of Machine Learning Models. IEEE TVCG 26(1):56–65. DOI:10.1109/TVCG.2019.2934619 · arXiv:1907.04135

    Contributes A widely deployed, low-code interface for counterfactual probing, feature analysis, and cross-model comparison.

    For FORGE The closest interaction-design precedent for the FORGE UX; it probes point predictions, not posterior structure — the gap FORGE fills.

Surveys & state-of-the-art positioning

  1. survey

    Gawlikowski, J., Njieutcheu Tassi, C.R., Ali, M., Lee, J., Humt, M., Feng, J., Kruspe, A., Triebel, R., Jung, P., Roscher, R., Shahzad, M., Yang, W., Bamler, R. & Zhu, X.X. (2023). A Survey of Uncertainty in Deep Neural Networks. Artificial Intelligence Review 56:1513–1589. DOI:10.1007/s10462-023-10562-9 · arXiv:2107.03342 2021 preprint; journal version 2023

    Contributes Comprehensive taxonomy of uncertainty sources (model vs. data) and quantification methods (Bayesian, ensemble, test-time, single-pass) plus calibration metrics.

    For FORGE The one-stop framework for organizing FORGE’s method coverage and labeling each panel’s uncertainty type.

  2. survey · position

    Papamarkou, T., Skoularidou, M., Palla, K., Aitchison, L., Arbel, J., Dunson, D., Filippone, M., Fortuin, V., Hennig, P., Hernández-Lobato, J.M., Hubin, A., Immer, A., Karaletsos, T., Khan, M.E., Kristiadi, A., Li, Y., Mandt, S., Nemeth, C., Osborne, M.A., Rudner, T.G.J., Rügamer, D., Teh, Y.W., Welling, M., Wilson, A.G. & Zhang, R. (2024). Position: Bayesian Deep Learning is Needed in the Age of Large-Scale AI. ICML 2024, PMLR 235:39556–39586. PMLR · arXiv:2402.00809 peer-reviewed position paper; arXiv v1 title differs

    Contributes A 25-author community statement arguing BDL’s value for uncertainty, continual/active learning, and scientific data, mapping research avenues toward scale.

    For FORGE The most current SOTA framing to motivate FORGE — its scientific-data and uncertainty arguments align with the HiggsML physics anchor.
